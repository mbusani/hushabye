What helped?
Settling coach
  1. Pause and listen for a minute or two. Some grizzles fade by themselves.
  2. Go in calmly. Keep the lights off or use the red light. Low, slow, boring voice and the same short phrase each time, like "Sleepy time, Mum's here."
  3. Check the basics. Nappy, too hot or cold, a sip of water, sore gums.
  4. Settle her in the cot first. Hand on her tummy, gentle shush or pat, sound playing. Stay close.
  5. If she's very upset, pick her up and cuddle until she's calm, not asleep. Then back in the cot, drowsy.
  6. Still awake after 30 to 45 minutes? Swap with the other parent if you can, and keep it dark and quiet. Some nights are just long. It isn't a failure.

    Sleep guide for 16 months

    How much sleep is normal
    • 11 to 14 hours in 24, counting naps. Most toddlers this age sleep about 10 to 12 hours at night.
    • One or two naps. Many move from two naps to one between 12 and 18 months. The changeover often upsets nights for a few weeks.
    • Waking once or twice a night is still common at this age. It's how quickly she gets back to sleep that matters most.
    Settling at bedtime
    • Same routine, same order, every night, about 30 to 45 minutes, ending in her room.
    • Watch the timing. Too long awake since her nap and she gets wired; too short and she isn't tired. Use the bedtime window above.
    • Into the cot drowsy but awake when you can. Falling asleep in the cot helps her resettle there at 2am.
    • Dim lights and screens off for the last half hour. A comforter like a soft toy can help from 12 months.
    When she won't go back down at night
    • Keep nights dark and boring. No lights, play or chatting, so night wakings aren't interesting.
    • Notice what she needs to fall asleep. If she's always fed, rocked or held to sleep at bedtime, she'll often need the same at every waking. The log's "what helped" shows this.
    • Change one thing slowly. For example, shorten a feed a little each night, or settle in the cot with a hand on her before picking up.
    • Both parents use the same plan for a week or two before deciding if it's working.
    • Check the room. Dark, quiet and not too warm, with light, comfortable sleepwear.
    Safe sleep
    • Follow Red Nose Australia: on her back to start, in her own cot, face and head uncovered.
    • No pillows, bumpers or loose bedding. Keep cords, blinds and heaters away from the cot.
    • A safe toddler sleeping bag is a good option at this age.
    When to get help
    • See your GP or child health nurse if she snores loudly most nights, pauses in her breathing, seems unwell or in pain, or if sleep problems carry on for weeks.
    • Get support for yourselves too. Ongoing broken sleep is hard. Ngala (WA) runs sleep programs and a parenting line: (08) 9368 9368.

      How to use Hushabye

      Hushabye helps you settle your little one at bedtime, get through night wakings, and spot what's working. Everything is on the tabs along the bottom.

      Getting set up

      1. Open your Hushabye link in Claude and sign in. A free Claude account is fine. Keep the link somewhere handy, like a pinned note.
      2. Add her name on the Ask tab and tap Save, so tips and suggestions are about her.
      3. Both parents can use it. When you're both invited, you see the same log live, so whoever is up at 2am knows what's already happened.

      At bedtime

      1. Check the bedtime window on the Log tab. It suggests the best time for bed based on when her nap ended.
      2. Follow the routine on the Routine tab and tick each step. You can add or remove steps to match what you do.
      3. Start a sound on the Sounds tab. Pink noise is a good start. Keep the volume low and the phone at least 2 metres from the cot. Choose "All night" or a fade-out time.
      4. When she's down, tap Asleep for the night on the Log tab.

      When she wakes in the night

      1. Tap Woke up on the Log tab.
      2. Follow the settling coach that appears. It moves to the next step the longer she's awake. Tap Red light for a dim glow, and double-tap the screen to turn it off.
      3. When she's back asleep, tick what helped (feed, cuddle, dummy and so on) and tap Back asleep.
      4. In the morning, tap Up for the day.

      During the day

      • Tap Nap started and Nap ended for each nap. That's what powers the bedtime window.
      • Read the recommendations on the Log tab. After two or three logged nights, they're based on her own pattern.
      • Browse the sleep guide at the bottom of the Log tab for what's normal at 16 months and what to try.

      Asking Claude

      • On the Ask tab, describe what's happening or tap a suggestion, like "What patterns do you see in our log?".
      • Claude reads your log and routine, so the more you log, the better the answers.
      • The first time, Claude asks your permission to answer. It uses your own Claude account.

      Handy to know

      • Logged something late? Tap its time in the list and change it. Tap Delete twice to remove an entry.
      • Change the look with the round button next to "How to use". Pick a theme, and choose light, dark, or Bedtime, which goes dark from 6 pm to 7 am.
      • Keep Hushabye open while a sound plays. The screen stays on, so use the red light to keep it dim.
      • Hushabye isn't medical advice. If she seems unwell, snores loudly most nights, or pauses in her breathing, see your GP or call healthdirect on 1800 022 222. In an emergency, call 000.
